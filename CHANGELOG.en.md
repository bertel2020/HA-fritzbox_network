# Changelog

All notable changes to this integration are recorded here.

## Unreleased

### Changed

- Taking over FRITZ!Box Tools: port forwardings are now matched to the new switches (by the description of the forwarding, as long as it fits exactly one). The entities of a repeater (restart, uptime, CPU temperature, Wi-Fi switches, guest Wi-Fi, QR code, firmware update) go to the entities of the repeater you chose under "Control repeaters". A repeater that is not controlled here stays "skipped", and the note now names that option.

## 1.5.0 – 2026-11-08

### New

- Panel: the charts (load, spectrum, data per day, the history charts of the device page) can be read with the keyboard. A chart takes the focus; the arrows, Home, End and Page Up/Down move from point to point, Esc hides the value. The value is in the tip and read out by screen readers.
- VPN page: switching a connection on or off is a switch like for Wi-Fi networks and routes (instead of a "Switch on/off" text button).
- New option **Check the certificate of the box** (at setup and under "Change the connection", off by default, with TLS only). When on, the connections to the box, to its web interface and to the capture check the certificate. The certificate of a FRITZ!Box is self-signed until you give it a trusted one, and valid only for its name; turn the option on only if the box is reachable under that name.
- VPN page: a tunnel that connects very often no longer loses its oldest history. Past 500 sessions the two with the smallest gap are joined, so the chart still reaches back a year and only the briefest breaks are drawn as connected.
- Panel: long grey hints under the cards are folded behind a small "Note" line (short ones stay). An opened note stays open when the page is redrawn.
- Panel: buttons have the same corner as input fields and range switches (rounded rectangle, 10 px) instead of a pill; round chips, tags and icon buttons keep their round shape.
- Panel: every card has the same head. The label is on the left, what belongs to the whole card (range switch, status, button) on the right, and the head has the same height everywhere, so cards side by side start on one line. On the Internet and Box pages the status moved into that head and the icon, name and details follow below it.
- The list of devices comes with 35 days of traffic again; the year is read only when you pick **Year** under data volume (new command `history/traffic`).
- Panel: lighter and more even. The sessions for the VPN chart are no longer sent with every update but read when the VPN page opens (new command `vpn/history`). All figure tiles share one look (same width, spacing, label above the value). Tips on charts stay open when the page is redrawn, charts keep their parsed data, and on touch screens small buttons are 44 px tall.
- Routes page: the counters are tiles like on the other pages (label above the number, a green tick when no gateway is offline). Each route names its target network first and the way it takes ("via gateway") with a dot for whether the gateway is reachable; the metric shows only when it is not 0. The state chip is gone (the switch says it), and delete is a trash icon. The VPN page counters are tiles in the same style.
- The time range switches are named the same everywhere: **Day, Week, Month, Year**. Device page (day and week, more is not recorded), data volume on the Internet page (now also Year; the daily traffic is kept for a year instead of 35 days), and the phone statistics (Day, Week, Month, Quarter, Year, All).
- VPN page: the **statistics** are now one continuous bar per WireGuard connection instead of a table. A range switch (day, week, month, year) sets how far it reaches. Blue is when the connection stood, light grey when it was on but not dialled in, striped when it was switched off. Hovering a bar names the state there and, for a connection, its start, end and duration. Whether a connection was switched on is noted from now on; the sessions are kept for a year.
- Internet page: the **Line quality** card is a table with line rate (and the possible rate), noise margin and attenuation in columns for download and upload, nothing is cut off any more. The noise margin has a dot (good from 10 dB, fine from 6 dB), the profile is a chip, and FEC and CRC errors are two tiles with a short explanation.
- Telephony page: the buttons under a call are equally wide in a grid, "Call again" is tinted as the main action, "Block" has a red outline, and "Add to phone book" and "Block" lose their dots.
- Internet page: the **Connection** card is tidier. Download and upload sit in two tiles, the three details (external IPv4, IPv6 prefix, last error) are equally wide with a copy button for the addresses, the status has a dot, and the DNS servers are listed in a column each for IPv4 and IPv6, with a copy button per server.
- Buttons now sit at the **right** of their card (rows of buttons and the buttons of the DHCP range form), as in the dialogs.
- Wi-Fi page: the **Guest access** card is calmer again. The switch sits at the right edge like in the box networks card, the line under the name says security and the number of devices, the device buttons appear only when there are devices, "QR code" turns into "Hide QR code" in the same row as "Change password", and the hint is one short line.
- Device page: the **signal chart** is coloured by strength (green from -60 dBm, yellow from -70, orange from -80, red below) on a fixed scale of -90 to -50 dBm, so the colour means the same at any time.
- Telephony page: the **phone book** is closed by default (a tap on the heading opens it). Open, it has filters (All, VIP, With calls), letter headings, the number of calls and the last call per contact, and a **New contact** button that also asks for the number.
- Telephony page: "Add to phone book" on a call can now **add the number to an existing contact** (as home, mobile, work or other) instead of always creating a new one. It needs the new action `add_number_to_contact` of `fritzbox_phone`; without it, and in privacy mode, only "new contact" is offered.
- Wi-Fi page: the "Guest access" card is tidier. It shows the security (for example WPA2 + WPA3) and the devices that are in the guest network right now as buttons that open their page. The QR code appears next to the details only on request, instead of an empty striped area; the long hint is small at the bottom.
- Repeaters you chose to control now also have a **Guest Wi-Fi** switch and the image **Guest Wi-Fi QR code** (off by default), as long as the repeater reports a guest network.
- Options: under **Create switches for** you choose the areas that get switches: devices (internet access, priority, Wake on LAN), Wi-Fi networks (box, repeaters, guest network and QR code), port forwardings, WireGuard connections, static routes. All are on by default. An area turned off removes its switches.

### Fixed

- Panel on a phone: the head is one row high, the sub pages (Devices, Wi-Fi, Address space, Routes) are one strip instead of four rows and fit at 320 px too. The status dot no longer covers the line under the title. Filter chips, range switches, links, switches and table heads have an area to touch of about 44 px. The bar of the Clean-up and Filter pages sits above the bar at the bottom (the "Remove" button was half hidden) and is smaller. The address grid has cells of 30 px and can be wiped sideways. On the phone page the figures sit two by two, and in the call list the badge (spam, answering machine) goes under the name instead of cutting the number.
- Several FRITZ!Boxes: the unique ids of the device entities (tracker, switches, sensors) now carry the serial number of the box. Before, they were the MAC only, so a device that two boxes know could be set up for one box only, and the actions (name, Wake on LAN) reached both boxes. Existing entries are renamed at start; entity ids, histories and automations stay. The actions reach only the box the chosen entity belongs to.
- Discovery (SSDP) no longer takes over the address of a known box, because any device of the network can fake the UDN (the public MAC) and the next login would go to it. Enter a new address under "Change the connection". The web login also refuses a challenge with an unusual number of rounds.
- Security: the four history commands of the panel (`history/summary`, `history/device`, `history/traffic`, `vpn/history`) now require an administrator like all the others.
- Security: the actions (set name, Wake on LAN, route, guest Wi-Fi password, capture, VPN clean-up) check the role of the calling user; only administrators may run them. Calls without a user, such as from automations, are still allowed.
- Switching the FRITZ!Box in the panel leaves nothing of the old box behind, and late answers of the old box are dropped. Address notes are sent only once they are loaded for the chosen box.
- CSV export: cells that start like a formula get a leading `'`, so a device name or note never runs as a formula in a spreadsheet.
- The tab lists take the arrow keys, Home and End; only the open tab is in the tab order.
- The bar at the bottom follows the width of the panel (compact up to 600 px) instead of the window width.
- The panel is told when the entry reloads for any reason (by hand, reauth, reconfigure), not only after changed options. Before, the topology stood still after that.
- Internet access and priority of a device without an IPv4 address are refused in the panel instead of reporting a success; a failure of the box comes back as an answer.
- The "offline for long" repair hides only the devices that are still offline for long when it is sent.
- The action `set_guest_wifi_password` requires printable ASCII like the panel. With several boxes, one without the right to change settings is skipped when another can be changed (`set_route`, `set_guest_wifi_password`).
- Port forwardings: if the box has no UPnP status information, the page still shows the forwardings set by hand.

## 1.4.0 – 2026-11-06

### New

- Dashboard: Two new cards. The **VPN card** (`custom:fritzbox-network-vpn-card`) shows the WireGuard connections as devices and links between sites with state, duration and a switch for each connection, plus the devices that are in the home network from outside (options `show_remote`, `only_active`). The **routes card** (`custom:fritzbox-network-routes-card`) shows the static routes as "target → gateway" with state, metric and a switch, and warns when the gateway of an enabled route is offline. ## 1.3.0 – 2026-11-06

### New

- Side panel, address space: New **Routes** page (address `#/routes`, switch at the top of the address page) with the static IPv4 routes of the box: destination network, gateway with the device from the list, metric and state (on, off, gateway offline), with a hint where the gateway of a switched-on route is offline. Each route can be switched on and off in the panel (the box is asked afterwards whether it took), and the action `fritzbox_network.set_route` (destination network, on/off) does it from automations. New routes can be added in the panel (destination network, gateway from the device list, metric) and deleted (with a confirmation). The input is checked while typing and again on the server: `0.0.0.0/0`, a destination that overlaps the home network or contains the box, a gateway outside the home network, the box as gateway, a gateway inside the destination and a network that already has a route are refused; for a gateway that is offline the form only warns. After adding and deleting, the integration reads the routes back and reports an error if the box did not take the change. Changing a route means deleting and adding it again. The box lists only the routes entered by hand, not those it sets up itself. Read when the page opens; checked on a FRITZ!Box 7590 AX (FRITZ!OS 8.25).
- Side panel, routes: Next to the list there is now a **Way of the traffic** card for the selected route: home network, box, gateway and destination network as a sketch (red where the gateway is offline; grey and dashed for a switched-off route) with state, gateway, interface and metric. It also says whether the gateway has a fixed address and warns if not (if the address changes, the route no longer fits). Routes are switched with a toggle; deleting is in each row of the list.
- Routes as entities: Each static route of the box is a switch "Route <network>" (attributes: network, gateway, device and state of the gateway, interface, metric) for dashboards, scenes and automations. The routes are read every 10 polls (about every 5 minutes) and at once after each change by the integration; new routes get their switch at the next read, switches of deleted routes go after two reads, or at once where the route is deleted in the panel. Only with the right "FRITZ!Box settings".

### Changed

- Side panel, Internet: The **Port forwardings** card is redesigned: tiles at the top (open to the internet, closed, devices) and filters (All, Open, Closed, IPv6, UPnP) with counts, below them the forwardings grouped by device (name as a link, address, online or offline). Each forwarding is one row with state (open, closed, partly open), name, chips for IPv4, IPv6 and UPnP, protocol and port; the same forwarding for IPv4 and IPv6 is one row. Open ones come first, closed ones are pale.

### Fixed

- Side panel, Internet: The **Port forwardings** card listed only the forwardings opened by UPnP. TR-064 does not know the ones set by hand in the box, so a box with forwardings set up showed "No ports are open to the internet". They are now read from the page "Port forwardings" of the web interface, also those for IPv6 and port ranges; an entry says whether it is for IPv6 or opened by UPnP. Where the box cannot deliver the page, the card says so instead of claiming that no ports are open.

## 1.2.0 – 2026-11-05

### New

- WireGuard (VPN): every connection of the box is a binary sensor "VPN <name>" (on while the tunnel stands; attributes: kind, enabled, connected since, remote network), and the side panel has a new **VPN** tab (`#/vpn`) with the devices and the links between sites, their state and the devices that come in by VPN. Read from the web interface of the box; the keys of the box on that page are never read. The new action `fritzbox_network.remove_unavailable_vpn` removes the entities of connections that are gone from the box. Each connection also has a **connected since** sensor and, off by default, a diagnostic **identifier** sensor (the place in the list of the box). Each connection has a switch "VPN <name> enabled" and a button on the VPN tab, for accounts that may change settings; the connection is read back and a change the box did not take is an error. The box numbers its connections by place, so the entities know a connection by its name (with the identifier added only where two share a name) and a deletion on the box does not move them to another connection; a renamed connection is a new entity. An empty answer of the box is believed only after three polls.
- Mesh: Each repeater has a **Mesh connection** binary sensor (on while the box lists it in its mesh; attributes: role, model, firmware, mesh version) that turns off when the box stops listing it, and a diagnostic **Uplink** sensor (what the repeater hangs on; attributes: connection, band, signal, link rates). The box has a **Mesh nodes** sensor (number of access points; attributes: repeaters, lost repeaters). A repeater the box has not listed for ten minutes is a repair; it goes away on its own, or you delete the device. The channel sensor of a radio tells network name, channel width, security and whether it carries the guest network. The side panel names a lost repeater in the hints and in the Wi-Fi check and shows firmware, channel width and guest network of the radios. A repeater stays a device of this integration while the box does not list it, even if a filter would otherwise hide it.
- Devices: **Vendor** diagnostic sensor (from the OUI list of Home Assistant; not for randomized MAC addresses), **Address assignment** diagnostic sensor ("Always the same" or "Handed out"; read from the web interface of the box, at most three devices per poll and each at most once an hour, only where the box answers). The IP address sensor tells as attributes whether the address is in the DHCP range, guest, VPN, interface, port and hostname.
- Box: **DHCP range in use** diagnostic sensor (percent of the addresses in the range that an active device has; attributes: first and last address, size of the range, in use, offline, free, first free address, known fixed addresses, subnet mask, gateway, reserved addresses) and, off by default, **External IPv6 address**.

- New option **Wake on LAN also for Wi-Fi devices** (off by default): otherwise the Wake on LAN button and the "Automatic Wake on LAN" switch exist only for devices not known to sit on Wi-Fi. Turning it off does not remove what was created.
- Side panel, Wi-Fi check: each access point shows the mesh version next to the firmware.
- Side panel, Wi-Fi check: each radio names the network (SSID) and the encryption (WPA2, WPA3, WPA2/WPA3).
- Side panel, device page: the **hostname** of the device is listed where it says something other than the name.
- Side panel, address space: the DHCP range tile names the **first free address** in the range.

### Changed

- Side panel, device page: The **Controls** card (internet access as a status bar, access profile, priority, Wake on LAN) switches at the box itself and no longer needs entities enabled in Home Assistant; the tools (rename, network map, capture) have their own **Device** card. The note about entities that are off is a side note there. New command `fritzbox_network/device/action` in the panel backend.
- Side panel, device page: The **data volume** (today, 7 and 30 days) is shown in tiles with a bar for the download and coloured arrows instead of three widely spread columns.
- Side panel: when the options change elsewhere (for example in the settings of the integration) and the box reloads, the panel takes its connection up again instead of standing still with old numbers. The open **Include** page takes the new filters; if you already have changes in the draft they stay, with a note that saving overwrites the other change.
- Side panel: the **Devices** tab (Include and Clean up) is now **Manage**, because the list of devices is the "Network" tab. The addresses `#/filter` and `#/cleanup` stay the same.
- The box and its repeaters have no switches for internet access, priority and automatic Wake on LAN, no Wake on LAN button, no "Access profile" selection and no "Access point" and "Last online" sensors: they are no clients, a block would hit everything behind them. Entities of earlier versions are removed at start.
- The WireGuard entities sit on a device of their own, **WireGuard VPN**, behind the box instead of on the device of the box; their names no longer start with "VPN" ("VPN Anna iPhone" becomes "WireGuard VPN Anna iPhone", the entity ids stay). The device cannot be deleted while the box lists connections; the action `remove_unavailable_vpn` takes it away when no connection is left.
- The repair "The internet connection keeps dropping" comes from 5 new connections in 24 hours (3 before), and so does the hint on the internet page of the panel.

## 1.1.0 – 2026-11-04

### New

- Side panel, Diagnosis: the **Wi-Fi check** lists each finding and shows every access point with its radios (channel, load, noise), the devices on them, those on a weak link and how a repeater is linked. The **event log** highlights radar events, DSL messages, failed logins, IPv6 prefix changes and internet connection messages, counts those of the last 24 hours and can be narrowed to them (found by the text of the message, German and English). In the packet capture the interfaces have plain names (whole home network, LAN port 1, guest network 5 GHz, WireGuard connection …), the recordings are cards in the list, and **Detect Wi-Fi bands** names the access points `vap0` and `vap1` after their band (from the devices packets are sent to; until then the usual 2.4 and 5 GHz are shown as "assumed"). The **course of the connection** runs the connection check every 5 minutes and shows the day as one strip per step (fast, slow, failed); it is off until you switch it on, and the times are kept for three days.
- Side panel: **Include** and **Clean up** are one tab, **Devices**, with a switch between the two pages; the count of old devices sits on the tab. The addresses `#/filter` and `#/cleanup` stay valid. Six pages now fit the header, and below 1180 px the tabs get a row of their own.
- Side panel: New **Diagnosis** page (address `#/diagnose`). The **packet capture** records the traffic of the box as a pcap (the panel reads the interfaces from the box: internet connections, LAN, Wi-Fi access points, WireGuard, routing; up to 10 minutes, size limit), keeps the last 20 files for download and analyses a file in the panel (protocols, biggest conversations, DNS names asked for). On the page of a device, "Record traffic" keeps only its traffic (by MAC and IP). The **connection check** tries the box, the internet, DNS, a website and IPv6 from Home Assistant and names the first step that fails. The **event log** of the box can be read by group. New action `fritzbox_network.capture` for automations. The interface list and the data format are checked on a FRITZ!Box 7590 AX (FRITZ!OS 8.25).

## 1.0.0 – 2026-11-02

### New

- Side panel: New **appearance** (the palette in the header). Three colour themes, **Ocean** (as before), **Forest** and **Graphite**, each light and dark; light or dark follows Home Assistant if wanted (the default) but can also be fixed. Plus three sizes of the type: Small (12.6 px), Normal (14 px, as before) and Large (16 px); all type in the panel scales with it, the smallest stays at 10.5 px or more. The choice is kept per browser. The status colours (ok, warning, error) are the same in every theme.
- Address space: **Used**, **Fixed addresses** and **Free** are one wider tile "Usage" with a bar of fixed, dynamic, offline and free addresses. The DHCP tile now also shows how many addresses in the range are free.
- Internet page: new card **Addresses** with every DNS server of the box (IPv4 and IPv6) and the **earlier external addresses** (IPv4, IPv6, IPv6 prefix) with "from … to …". The events of the connection (lost, set up again, new address) and the addresses are now kept over restarts of Home Assistant (up to 200 events, 20 addresses per kind).
- Side panel: new page **Address space** (button in the header of the main page, address `#/addresses`). It shows the /24 around the box as a map of 256 tiles (dynamic, fixed, offline, free, reserved, planned) or as a list, with filters and search, tiles for network, gateway, DHCP range, usage, fixed and free addresses, the split into fixed zones and DHCP range, and things that stand out (duplicate addresses, a fixed address inside the DHCP range, a reservation without a device, offline devices with a fixed address, an almost full pool, DHCP off). A click on an address shows device, type, area and connection.
- Own entries in the address space: a note per address, a display name per device, "planned" for free addresses, own blocks with name and colour (with usage) and hints that can be hidden. They are kept in the `.storage` of Home Assistant and change nothing in the box. New websocket commands `address/overview`, `address/fixed` and `address/save`, all for administrators only.
- Address space: **export and print**. CSV (semicolon, UTF-8 with a mark for Excel), JSON (network, numbers, every address and the own entries, also usable as a backup) and a **print sheet for A4 portrait** that opens in a window of its own and is printed there or saved as a file with "Save as PDF": a head with the date, key figures, a map of the addresses (with patterns, readable in black and white), things that stand out, the division, own blocks and the list of addresses in use with a header on every page and page numbers. Options: free addresses too (CSV, JSON), MAC addresses, the map on the sheet. Everything is made in the browser.
- Address space: the **DHCP range** can be changed (card at the bottom of the page, only with write rights of the box account). The preview first shows how many addresses the range has, which dynamic devices lie outside it (they keep their address until they renew it) and which fixed devices lie inside. A dialog asks before anything is written. New websocket command `address/pool` (`SetAddressRange`), which checks the range: inside the network, in order, neither the network or broadcast address nor the box.
- Whether an address is fixed is read from the web interface of the box, a few devices per request and cached for ten minutes. Where the box does not say, the addresses show as dynamic, with a hint.

- Side panel, device page: **data volume** per device (today, 7 and 30 days, download and upload). The integration reads the per-device internet counters from the mesh topology of the box, adds up the difference per day (a counter that was reset starts anew) and keeps 35 days in `.storage`. The history starts with the first poll after the update. `history/device` and `history/summary` also return `traffic`.
- Side panel: the table has the sortable column **Volume (7 d)**, the internet page the ranking **Data volume per device** (today, 7 or 30 days, the ten largest devices with bars and the sum of all devices).
- Side panel: the charts show time and value on hover (or tap). The LAN icon is a network now instead of a plug. An **Internet** button in the header of the main page opens the internet page.
- Side panel: the list is **flat** by default and a sortable table (click the column heads) with connection, rate, signal, a sparkline of the last 24 hours and "Last online", with pages (50 rows, choose 25/50/100/all, the panel remembers the choice). Grouping by access point, band and group stays available.
- The integration keeps online times, signal and rate per device (a day in steps of five minutes, a week in hours) in the `.storage` of Home Assistant, so the sparklines survive a restart. New websocket commands `history/summary` and `history/device`.
- Side panel, device page: history of signal and rate over 24 hours or 7 days with the lowest, mean and highest value.
- Side panel: a network map as a second view next to the list (switch **List | Network map**, the panel remembers the choice). The FRITZ!Box and every repeater are areas of their own, the devices hang as buttons with initials on lines for LAN and the Wi-Fi bands. The line to a repeater shows whether it is linked by cable or Wi-Fi. Filtered devices are left out of the map, with the note "N hidden by the filter".
- The topology adds `uplink` per repeater: type, band and signal of its link, from the mesh topology.
- An action whose entity is switched off in Home Assistant shows the known value (access profile, priority) and an **Enable** button, with two or more also **Enable all**. Home Assistant then reloads the integration briefly.

- Side panel, internet page: redesigned. The connection on top with a tinted header, below it side by side the load (large live values for download and upload, the course with a tooltip per point) and the **data volume** (today, 7 and 30 days, bars per day with a tooltip, the ten most used devices as a ranking with their download and upload share).
- Side panel, device page: next to the IP address it says **fixed** or **automatic**, depending on whether the box always gives the device the same address (reservation). The integration reads it from the web interface of the box (`data.lua`, with the account of the integration) when the page opens, because TR-064 does not offer it. New websocket command `device/ip`, for administrators only. Where the box says nothing, the label stays away.

### Changed

- Side panel: the header follows the look of the other integrations: the **logo** of the integration and the title on the left, the pages as pills (the active one filled) and on the right the status with the **Integration** button, which opens the settings of the integration in Home Assistant. On a phone logo, status and button stay in one row and the pages scroll below. The integration serves the logo itself (`brand/icon.png`).

- Side panel: new header. **Internet** and **Address space** are tabs now, next to Network, Clean up and Include, instead of buttons at the top right. A dot on the "Internet" tab shows whether the line is connected. The status pill is a small text with a dot ("19 s ago"), and the back arrows on both pages are gone.

- Side panel: the vendor of a device (from its MAC address) is always shown, even when the option "Show the vendor of a device" is off. The option only controls the manufacturer of the device in Home Assistant and in the new device event. A private MAC address says "none (private MAC address)" instead of "unknown".

- Side panel: **Clean up** and **Filter** are rebuilt. The "Filter" tab is now called **Include**, because it decides for which devices Home Assistant creates devices and entities, not what the panel shows. Include: the three modes as explained cards, the two extra rules, the device list with a counter and "Select shown" and "Select none", and a bar at the bottom with "stay / come back / are removed" and the button **Apply changes**. Clean up: the longest offline first with the duration, search, "Select all" and "Select none" and an action bar that stays in view. "Hide" is now **Remove** (it removes the devices with their entities from Home Assistant), the removed devices sit in a fold-out section and can be **added again**.

- Side panel: the pages are underlined tabs at the left under the title. The lines of the connection path sit at the same height, with the label below. "Clean up" takes fixed steps (3, 7, 14, 30, 90 days, 1 year) as buttons instead of the slider. The group "No access point" is now "Not assigned".
- Side panel: a device opens as a page of its own instead of a column on the right. Back leads to the list or the map with filter, scroll position and focus; ‹ › or `j`/`k` page through the devices shown. New at the top of the page is the connection path (internet, FRITZ!Box, repeater, device); from 900 px the actions and the master data stand side by side.
- Side panel: instead of a long row of filters there are three fixed ones (All, Online, Offline) and the "More" menu with the other filters and the groups, on a phone as a sheet from the bottom. The row and the radio boxes wrap instead of scrolling sideways.
- Access profile: the built-in profiles of the box come without a name; they are now called "Standard", "Guest" and "Unrestricted" after their type instead of "filtprof1", "filtprof2" and "filtprof3".

## 0.1.0 – 2026-10-25

First version.

### New

- The network devices of the FRITZ!Box as Home Assistant devices, tracked by MAC address. Entity ids never change, the device name follows the name in the box, names you set yourself are kept.
  - Presence, IP address, connection type (LAN/WLAN), access point, "online since" and "last online".
  - Block and allow the internet access per device, Wake on LAN.
  - Optional, off by default: signal strength, link rate, Wi-Fi band, network name and a guest network flag.
  - The FRITZ!Box and its repeaters as devices of their own with model, firmware and the number of connected devices.
- Tidying up: delete offline devices by hand (they stay hidden), filters for the guest network, private MAC addresses and single devices, and an option not to create devices for offline hosts. Repeaters are never filtered.
- Side panel for administrators: the whole network in the sidebar with status filters and counts, search, grouping, radio data per access point and a detail page per device (block internet, priority, access profile, Wake on LAN, rename). Plus groups through Home Assistant labels (filter, grouping, block all, option `label` on the devices card, input "Group" in the schedule blueprint), links to the logbook and the history on the device page, an internet page (connection, addresses, load with a course, DSL line quality with the signal over the frequency, notes on anything unusual, recent events), plus sensors for the connection, the throughput and the DSL line, the port forwardings of the box (view only), an update entity for the firmware of the box, a switch for the guest Wi-Fi, repairs for a line that stays weak or a connection that keeps dropping, notes on the network in the panel (a busy radio, a weakly linked repeater), actions "Reconnect" and "Restart the box" in the panel and events (also in the logbook) for a lost and a new connection and a new external IPv4, bulk clean-up of long-offline devices and the device filter with a preview that shows what would be removed with its entities.
- Dashboard cards: a Wi-Fi card (channel, load, noise and device count per radio of every access point) and a devices card (chosen devices with block or allow internet, Wake on LAN and one button for all). Topology card for the dashboard: the devices by access point at a glance, with online dot, link, signal and a warning for a weak link. A tap on a device opens its page in the side panel, a tap on the heading the overview; search, filters, details and actions are there. It has a visual editor (title, collapsed access points, maximum height, threshold for weak links), loads itself and needs an administrator account.
- More switches and a select, off by default: access profile, priority and automatic Wake on LAN per device. Optional: the vendor from the MAC address, without any network access.
- Actions `set_name` and `wake_on_lan`, an event when a device changes its access point, logbook entries for all events and, optionally, a repair that offers to hide devices that have been offline for long.
- Setup: a new address and a new password without setting up again, a mesh repeater is refused, diagnostics with the poll duration.
