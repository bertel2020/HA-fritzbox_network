# Changelog

All notable changes to this integration are recorded here.

## 1.2.0 – 2026-11-04

### New

- WireGuard (VPN): every connection of the box is a binary sensor "VPN <name>" (on while the tunnel stands; attributes: kind, enabled, connected since, remote network), and the side panel has a new **VPN** tab (`#/vpn`) with the devices and the links between sites, their state and the devices that come in by VPN. Read from the web interface of the box; the keys of the box on that page are never read. Each connection has a switch "VPN <name> enabled" and a button on the VPN tab, for accounts that may change settings; the connection is read back and a change the box did not take is an error. The box numbers its connections by place, so the entities know a connection by its name (with the identifier added only where two share a name) and a deletion on the box does not move them to another connection; a renamed connection is a new entity. An empty answer of the box is believed only after three polls.

## 1.1.0 – 2026-11-04

### New

- Side panel, Diagnosis: the **Wi-Fi check** lists each finding and shows every access point with its radios (channel, load, noise), the devices on them, those on a weak link and how a repeater is linked. The **event log** highlights radar events, DSL messages, failed logins, IPv6 prefix changes and internet connection messages, counts those of the last 24 hours and can be narrowed to them (found by the text of the message, German and English). In the packet capture the interfaces have plain names (whole home network, LAN port 1, guest network 5 GHz, WireGuard connection …), the recordings are cards in the list, and **Detect Wi-Fi bands** names the access points `vap0` and `vap1` after their band (from the devices packets are sent to; until then the usual 2.4 and 5 GHz are shown as "assumed"). The **course of the connection** runs the connection check every 5 minutes and shows the day as one strip per step (fast, slow, failed); it is off until you switch it on, and the times are kept for three days.
- Side panel: **Include** and **Clean up** are one tab, **Devices**, with a switch between the two pages; the count of old devices sits on the tab. The addresses `#/filter` and `#/cleanup` stay valid. Six pages now fit the header, and below 1180 px the tabs get a row of their own.
- Side panel: New **Diagnosis** page (address `#/diagnose`). The **packet capture** records the traffic of the box as a pcap (the panel reads the interfaces from the box: internet connections, LAN, Wi-Fi access points, WireGuard, routing; up to 10 minutes, size limit), keeps the last 20 files for download and analyses a file in the panel (protocols, biggest conversations, DNS names asked for). On the page of a device, "Record traffic" keeps only its traffic (by MAC and IP). The **connection check** tries the box, the internet, DNS, a website and IPv6 from Home Assistant and names the first step that fails. The **event log** of the box can be read by group. New action `fritzbox_network.capture` for automations. The interface list and the data format are checked on a FRITZ!Box 7590 AX (FRITZ!OS 8.25).

## 1.0.0 – 2026-11-02

### New

- Side panel: New **appearance** (the palette in the header). Three colour themes, **Ocean** (as before), **Forest** and **Graphite**, each light and dark; light or dark follows Home Assistant if wanted (the default) but can also be fixed. Plus three sizes of the type: Small (12.6 px), Normal (14 px, as before) and Large (16 px); all type in the panel scales with it, the smallest stays at 10.5 px or more. The choice is kept per browser. The status colours (ok, warning, error) are the same in every theme.
- Address space: **Used**, **Fixed addresses** and **Free** are one wider tile "Usage" with a bar of fixed, dynamic, offline and free addresses. The DHCP tile now also shows how many addresses in the range are free.
- Internet page: new card **Addresses** with every DNS server of the box (IPv4 and IPv6) and the **earlier external addresses** (IPv4, IPv6, IPv6 prefix) with "from … to …". The events of the connection (lost, set up again, new address) and the addresses are now kept over restarts of Home Assistant (up to 200 events, 20 addresses per kind).
- Side panel: new page **Address space** (button in the header of the main page, address `#/addresses`). It shows the /24 around the box as a map of 256 tiles (dynamic, fixed, offline, free, reserved, planned) or as a list, with filters and search, tiles for network, gateway, DHCP range, usage, fixed and free addresses, the split into fixed zones and DHCP range, and things that stand out (duplicate addresses, a fixed address inside the DHCP range, a reservation without a device, offline devices with a fixed address, an almost full pool, DHCP off). A click on an address shows device, type, area and connection.
- Own entries in the address space: a note per address, a display name per device, "planned" for free addresses, own blocks with name and colour (with usage) and hints that can be hidden. They are kept in the `.storage` of Home Assistant and change nothing in the box. New websocket commands `address/overview`, `address/fixed` and `address/save`, all for administrators only.
- Address space: **export and print**. CSV (semicolon, UTF-8 with a mark for Excel), JSON (network, numbers, every address and the own entries, also usable as a backup) and a **print sheet for A4 portrait** that opens in a window of its own and is printed there or saved as a file with "Save as PDF": a head with the date, key figures, a map of the addresses (with patterns, readable in black and white), things that stand out, the division, own blocks and the list of addresses in use with a header on every page and page numbers. Options: free addresses too (CSV, JSON), MAC addresses, the map on the sheet. Everything is made in the browser.
- Address space: the **DHCP range** can be changed (card at the bottom of the page, only with write rights of the box account). The preview first shows how many addresses the range has, which dynamic devices lie outside it (they keep their address until they renew it) and which fixed devices lie inside. A dialog asks before anything is written. New websocket command `address/pool` (`SetAddressRange`), which checks the range: inside the network, in order, neither the network or broadcast address nor the box.
- Whether an address is fixed is read from the web interface of the box, a few devices per request and cached for ten minutes. Where the box does not say, the addresses show as dynamic, with a hint.

- Side panel, device page: **data volume** per device (today, 7 and 30 days, download and upload). The integration reads the per-device internet counters from the mesh topology of the box, adds up the difference per day (a counter that was reset starts anew) and keeps 35 days in `.storage`. The history starts with the first poll after the update. `history/device` and `history/summary` also return `traffic`.
- Side panel: the table has the sortable column **Volume (7 d)**, the internet page the ranking **Data volume per device** (today, 7 or 30 days, the ten largest devices with bars and the sum of all devices).
- Side panel: the charts show time and value on hover (or tap). The LAN icon is a network now instead of a plug. An **Internet** button in the header of the main page opens the internet page.
- Side panel: the list is **flat** by default and a sortable table (click the column heads) with connection, rate, signal, a sparkline of the last 24 hours and "Last online", with pages (50 rows, choose 25/50/100/all, the panel remembers the choice). Grouping by access point, band and group stays available.
- The integration keeps online times, signal and rate per device (a day in steps of five minutes, a week in hours) in the `.storage` of Home Assistant, so the sparklines survive a restart. New websocket commands `history/summary` and `history/device`.
- Side panel, device page: history of signal and rate over 24 hours or 7 days with the lowest, mean and highest value.
- Side panel: a network map as a second view next to the list (switch **List | Network map**, the panel remembers the choice). The FRITZ!Box and every repeater are areas of their own, the devices hang as buttons with initials on lines for LAN and the Wi-Fi bands. The line to a repeater shows whether it is linked by cable or Wi-Fi. Filtered devices are left out of the map, with the note "N hidden by the filter".
- The topology adds `uplink` per repeater: type, band and signal of its link, from the mesh topology.
- An action whose entity is switched off in Home Assistant shows the known value (access profile, priority) and an **Enable** button, with two or more also **Enable all**. Home Assistant then reloads the integration briefly.

- Side panel, internet page: redesigned. The connection on top with a tinted header, below it side by side the load (large live values for download and upload, the course with a tooltip per point) and the **data volume** (today, 7 and 30 days, bars per day with a tooltip, the ten most used devices as a ranking with their download and upload share).
- Side panel, device page: next to the IP address it says **fixed** or **automatic**, depending on whether the box always gives the device the same address (reservation). The integration reads it from the web interface of the box (`data.lua`, with the account of the integration) when the page opens, because TR-064 does not offer it. New websocket command `device/ip`, for administrators only. Where the box says nothing, the label stays away.

### Changed

- Side panel: the header follows the look of the other integrations: the **logo** of the integration and the title on the left, the pages as pills (the active one filled) and on the right the status with the **Integration** button, which opens the settings of the integration in Home Assistant. On a phone logo, status and button stay in one row and the pages scroll below. The integration serves the logo itself (`brand/icon.png`).

- Side panel: new header. **Internet** and **Address space** are tabs now, next to Network, Clean up and Include, instead of buttons at the top right. A dot on the "Internet" tab shows whether the line is connected. The status pill is a small text with a dot ("19 s ago"), and the back arrows on both pages are gone.

- Side panel: the vendor of a device (from its MAC address) is always shown, even when the option "Show the vendor of a device" is off. The option only controls the manufacturer of the device in Home Assistant and in the new device event. A private MAC address says "none (private MAC address)" instead of "unknown".

- Side panel: **Clean up** and **Filter** are rebuilt. The "Filter" tab is now called **Include**, because it decides for which devices Home Assistant creates devices and entities, not what the panel shows. Include: the three modes as explained cards, the two extra rules, the device list with a counter and "Select shown" and "Select none", and a bar at the bottom with "stay / come back / are removed" and the button **Apply changes**. Clean up: the longest offline first with the duration, search, "Select all" and "Select none" and an action bar that stays in view. "Hide" is now **Remove** (it removes the devices with their entities from Home Assistant), the removed devices sit in a fold-out section and can be **added again**.

- Side panel: the pages are underlined tabs at the left under the title. The lines of the connection path sit at the same height, with the label below. "Clean up" takes fixed steps (3, 7, 14, 30, 90 days, 1 year) as buttons instead of the slider. The group "No access point" is now "Not assigned".
- Side panel: a device opens as a page of its own instead of a column on the right. Back leads to the list or the map with filter, scroll position and focus; ‹ › or `j`/`k` page through the devices shown. New at the top of the page is the connection path (internet, FRITZ!Box, repeater, device); from 900 px the actions and the master data stand side by side.
- Side panel: instead of a long row of filters there are three fixed ones (All, Online, Offline) and the "More" menu with the other filters and the groups, on a phone as a sheet from the bottom. The row and the radio boxes wrap instead of scrolling sideways.
- Access profile: the built-in profiles of the box come without a name; they are now called "Standard", "Guest" and "Unrestricted" after their type instead of "filtprof1", "filtprof2" and "filtprof3".

## 0.1.0 – 2026-10-25

First version.

### New

- The network devices of the FRITZ!Box as Home Assistant devices, tracked by MAC address. Entity ids never change, the device name follows the name in the box, names you set yourself are kept.
  - Presence, IP address, connection type (LAN/WLAN), access point, "online since" and "last online".
  - Block and allow the internet access per device, Wake on LAN.
  - Optional, off by default: signal strength, link rate, Wi-Fi band, network name and a guest network flag.
  - The FRITZ!Box and its repeaters as devices of their own with model, firmware and the number of connected devices.
- Tidying up: delete offline devices by hand (they stay hidden), filters for the guest network, private MAC addresses and single devices, and an option not to create devices for offline hosts. Repeaters are never filtered.
- Side panel for administrators: the whole network in the sidebar with status filters and counts, search, grouping, radio data per access point and a detail page per device (block internet, priority, access profile, Wake on LAN, rename). Plus groups through Home Assistant labels (filter, grouping, block all, option `label` on the devices card, input "Group" in the schedule blueprint), links to the logbook and the history on the device page, an internet page (connection, addresses, load with a course, DSL line quality with the signal over the frequency, notes on anything unusual, recent events), plus sensors for the connection, the throughput and the DSL line, the port forwardings of the box (view only), an update entity for the firmware of the box, a switch for the guest Wi-Fi, repairs for a line that stays weak or a connection that keeps dropping, notes on the network in the panel (a busy radio, a weakly linked repeater), actions "Reconnect" and "Restart the box" in the panel and events (also in the logbook) for a lost and a new connection and a new external IPv4, bulk clean-up of long-offline devices and the device filter with a preview that shows what would be removed with its entities.
- Dashboard cards: a Wi-Fi card (channel, load, noise and device count per radio of every access point) and a devices card (chosen devices with block or allow internet, Wake on LAN and one button for all). Topology card for the dashboard: the devices by access point at a glance, with online dot, link, signal and a warning for a weak link. A tap on a device opens its page in the side panel, a tap on the heading the overview; search, filters, details and actions are there. It has a visual editor (title, collapsed access points, maximum height, threshold for weak links), loads itself and needs an administrator account.
- More switches and a select, off by default: access profile, priority and automatic Wake on LAN per device. Optional: the vendor from the MAC address, without any network access.
- Actions `set_name` and `wake_on_lan`, an event when a device changes its access point, logbook entries for all events and, optionally, a repair that offers to hide devices that have been offline for long.
- Setup: a new address and a new password without setting up again, a mesh repeater is refused, diagnostics with the poll duration.
